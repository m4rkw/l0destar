# v3.4 CAN interface bench test

**The CAN interface on the v3.4 board, which replaces the MAX33041E transceiver with a TCAN3414DR, passes the same bench suite the v3.1 board did: about 60 000 frames in classic and FD modes, 125 kbps to 8 Mbps, with no corruption and no unprovoked errors up to and including 5 Mbps (two retried bit errors at 8 Mbps against the adapter, the same count as before; the board's own loopback at 8 Mbps is clean). On the oscilloscope the new part gives the same 2.0 V dominant differential with 30 ns edges in place of the MAX33041E's sub-10 ns ones, half the undershoot, and a loop delay of 86 / 107 ns, all inside its datasheet. Wake-on-bus through its wake-up-pattern standby works with the existing firmware.**

*MCP2518FD + TCAN3414DR · 19 September 2026 · [source, scripts and raw results in the firmware repository](https://github.com/m4rkw/l0destar) · the v3.1 board with the MAX33041E: [v3.1 CAN interface bench test](https://github.com/m4rkw/l0destar/blob/master/firmware/CAN_BENCH_REPORT.md)*

**This has not been tested in a vehicle yet (my car doesn't have CAN).**

## Setup

Two-node bus on the bench: the v3.4 board's TCAN3414DR at one end, a DSD TECH SH-C31A (CANable 2.0, STM32G431) at the other, 120 Ω at both ends, short cable, board powered over USB-C. The CAN circuit is otherwise the v3.1 design: MCP2518FD on a 40 MHz crystal, NUP2105L protector, 220 Ω series resistors on CAN_SDI/CAN_SCK and 1 kΩ on CAN_CS, the transceiver's STB pin driven by the controller's GPIO0 through XSTBYEN. The board runs a test agent (`src/can_bench.c`, `CONFIG_APP_CAN_BENCH=y`, board profile `APP_BOARD_L0DESTAR_V3_4`) that is driven from the host over the bus itself, so every command and reply is also a CAN exchange. Very short wires are soldered to the TCAN3414DR: CH1 CANH, CH2 CANL, CH3 TXD, CH4 RXD, with a common ground at the chip's GND pin.

- **Firmware agent** — owns the MCP2518FD. Counts and sequence-checks frames, echoes on ID+1, bursts, changes mode and timing, loopbacks, sleeps, cycles the rail, benchmarks the SPI link, reports TEC/REC and diagnostics.
- **Bit-banged SPI, two speeds** — production method (Zephyr GPIO API + 1 µs waits, ~130 kbit/s) or direct nrf_gpio with no waits (~890 kbit/s), switchable at run time.
- **Adapter** — the community gs_usb CAN FD fork (`tymmothy/candleLight_fw_canable_v2_fd` v1.0.1); the shipped candleLight build has no FD. Its quirks (128-byte padded USB frames, retry-forever jams, echo-on-queue, 160 MHz clock) are worked around in the host driver. Its own transceiver is a 5 V part: dominant CANH 3.5 V, CANL 1.4 V on the scope against the board's 3.0 / 1.0 V.
- **Host software** — pyusb gs_usb driver with FD and async USB, the adapter server on a local socket, and the test suite `can_bench/run_all.py`.

## Findings

### Hardware: nothing to fix

- **Transceiver.** FD data phase at 1, 2, 4, 5 and 8 Mbps in both directions with clean counters to 5 Mbps (D2, D3), and the board's own external loopback through the TCAN3414DR is error-free at every rate including 8 Mbps (D6). At 8 Mbps against the adapter, two data-phase bit errors on the board's own transmissions were retried and every frame delivered, which is exactly what the MAX33041E did in the same test. Since the board alone is clean at 8 Mbps and the errors only appear with the adapter's 5 V, 5 Mbps-class transceiver on the other end of the bus, they belong to the two-transceiver bus at a rate above anything a vehicle uses, not to the new part. The scope confirms the driver is well inside its datasheet (below).
- **Standby and wake-on-bus.** The TCAN3414DR's standby is different in kind from the MAX33041E's: the bus is weakly biased to ground, the main receiver is off, and RXD stays high until the low-power bus monitor has seen a complete ISO 11898-2:2016 wake-up pattern (filtered dominant, filtered recessive, filtered dominant, each longer than tWK_FILTER of 0.5–1.8 µs, all within tWK_TIMEOUT of 0.8–6 ms). Only then does RXD go low on the next filtered dominant. The datasheet says any classic frame at 500 kbps or slower contains a valid pattern, and E6 confirms it: with the controller in Sleep and STB high, the first 500 kbps frame set WAKIF and pulled CAN_INT low, 3 ms after the host's send timestamp. The existing `hw_can.c` sleep path (XSTBYEN, GPIO0) needs no change for the new part. Two things to remember: a 1 Mbps bus needs two back-to-back dominant bits to satisfy the filter, and the frame that wakes the transceiver is not received by the controller, which is also true of the MAX33041E and of the MCP2518FD's own wake behaviour.
- **SPI through the series resistors.** 10 240 bytes of message RAM written and read back with five patterns plus 200 timed transactions, zero errors, at both the production timing (131 kbit/s on the wire) and the direct-GPIO timing (894 kbit/s). The 220 Ω and 1 kΩ resistors do not limit the link at these speeds.
- **Controller and crystal.** Every bit rate from 125 k to 1 M works with clean counters. The adapter could be pulled 2.44 % low or 2.56 % high before the link broke, symmetric to within the ±0.3 % resolution of the test points, which rules out a gross crystal error but says nothing at the ppm level. Host sample points from 60 % to 95 % all worked against the board's 80 %.
- **Rail switching.** PP3V3_CAN drops below the sense threshold 22 ms after CAN_EN goes low and the controller is back in Configuration mode with its oscillator running 24–25 ms after it goes high, five times out of five.

### The SPI driver is the throughput limit

| direction, frame | production SPI | direct GPIO |
|---|---|---|
| receive, 8-byte classic | 500 frames/s lossless, overflow from ~550/s | 3340 frames/s, overflow from ~3750/s |
| transmit, 8-byte classic | 320 frames/s (375 with DLC 0) | 2150 frames/s (2535 with DLC 0) |
| receive, 64-byte FD | 500 frames/s | 2900 frames/s (bus limited) |
| transmit, 64-byte FD | 152 frames/s | 1050 frames/s |
| echo round trip | 6.6 ms median | 2.6 ms median |

These are the v3.1 numbers within run-to-run noise, which is the point: the transceiver is not in the path that limits any of them. A saturated 500 kbps bus carries about 4000 to 4400 eight-byte standard-ID frames a second; passenger-car powertrain buses run at 40 to 80 % load, 1600 to 3500 frames/s. With the production driver the 32-frame RX FIFO fills in 8 to 9 ms of bus-rate traffic and frames are lost, with the overflow flag raised reliably. Direct GPIO with no waits reaches roughly 75 % of the bus maximum with identical data integrity. Whatever the driver, hardware acceptance filters (E4) should reduce the load to the IDs of interest and the RX FIFO should be the full 32 objects, not the 4 used by the boot-time test.

### Mode choice on a vehicle bus

- **FD tolerance.** A controller in Normal CAN 2.0 mode error-flags every FD frame (D5), destroying it for every node on the bus while its own receive error counter climbs. Normal FD mode accepted classic frames in every test, so it is the safer default; Listen-only is safest if the tracker only observes.
- **Listen-only** is genuinely passive (E3). On the two-node bench a lone transmitter's frames only become valid to the passive listener once that transmitter is error-passive; on a real bus other nodes ACK.
- **One-shot transmit** works, but an aborted message stays in the FIFO with TXREQ clear and blocks everything behind it until the FIFO is reset (E1).
- **Error counter arithmetic (E1).** With no partner and retry on, TEC climbs by 8 per attempt until 128, where the error-passive exception stops ACK errors counting. It read 126 once the partner was back: the ceiling less the two frames delivered before the read. After the following burst it read 15, a fall of 111, which is the 9-frame reply to the first read, the 100 burst frames and the 2-frame burst reply: one decrement per successful transmission, as the specification says. The one-shot phase's TEC of 88 is 11 single attempts at 8 each.

## Oscilloscope

Rigol DHO814, 10× probes on flying leads soldered to the transceiver's CANH, CANL, TXD, RXD and GND pins, 500 mV/div on the bus channels. Whole 1 Mpt records were read over SCPI and every single-bit-wide run in the data phase was measured; the differential was recomputed from the raw samples. Board-transmit shows the TCAN3414DR's driver; adapter-transmit shows what the board has to decode. Levels are read 75 % into the bit, the earliest sample point in use on either side (board 80 % nominal and at 2 Mbps, 75 % at 5 Mbps; adapter 80 % nominal, 75 % data phase). In the board-transmit captures the adapter's ACK bit is also present, one dominant bit per frame from the far end at 3.5 / 1.4 V single-ended, so the persistence eyes show both drivers.

| FD 5 Mbps, board TX | 495 | 1.96 / 1.99 V | 0 V | +2.17 / −0.36 V | ±13 ns | 27 ns |

Single-ended, board transmitting: recessive CANH = CANL = 2.2 V; dominant CANH 2.9–3.0 V, CANL 1.0 V, a 2.0 V differential that the datasheet puts at 1.5–3.0 V into 60 Ω. The differential edges measure 26 to 31 ns 10–90 %, in the datasheet's 30–57 ns rise and 30–50 ns fall bands and the scope's own resolution is part of that; the MAX33041E's were under 10 ns. Each dominant-to-recessive transition still has a brief undershoot, now to about −0.36 V rather than −0.65 V, and a short ring that appears equally on both lines and cancels in the difference. Slower edges and half the undershoot is the direction that helps on a real harness, and it costs nothing at 5 Mbps: a 200 ns bit is flat within 150 mV about 40 ns after its edge, so the level is settled for 110 ns before a 75 % sample point. The TCAN3414DR has no slew-rate-limited mode, so this is the only driver it has. After every dominant bit both lines drop together to about 1.4–1.6 V and drift back to 2.2 V over several microseconds with zero differential: the receiver bias network recharging, normal, and confusing on a single-ended trace.

### Transceiver loop delay

Probes on the TCAN3414DR TXD and RXD pins, 232 edges each way at 500 kbps, spread under ±2 ns.

| edge | TXD → bus | bus → RXD | TXD → RXD | datasheet, TXD → RXD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| recessive → dominant | 40 ns | 46 ns | 86 ns | 95 typ, 180 max |
| dominant → recessive | 53 ns | 54 ns | 107 ns | 120 typ, 180 max |

A little faster than typical and well inside the maximum. The dominant bit starts 40 ns late on the bus and ends 53 ns late, so it is lengthened by 13 ns, 6.5 % of a 5 Mbps bit; the datasheet's pulse skew is 18 ns typical, 28 ns maximum, and the MAX33041E measured 13 ns the other way (shortened). The single-bit widths in the table above show it directly: dominant bits at 5 Mbps measure up to 213 ns and recessive ones down to 187 ns. Normal for a CAN FD transceiver, and the MCP2518FD's TDC in auto mode measures the loop delay itself; these are the numbers for a manual offset if auto is ever disabled.

## MAX33041E vs TCAN3414DR

Both are 3.3 V CAN FD transceivers in the same SOIC-8 pinout with an active-high standby pin, and the board is otherwise unchanged. What differs, from the datasheets and from the bench:

|  | MAX33041E (v3.1) | TCAN3414DR (v3.4) |
|---|---|---|
| Rated data rate | 5 Mbps | 2 / 5 Mbps (EMC certified), 8 Mbps in simple networks; bit-time symmetry specified at all three |
| Dominant differential at the sample point, board TX, worst of ~500 bits at 5 Mbps | 1.81 V | 1.96 V |
| Differential edge, 10–90 % | <10 ns (scope-limited) | 27–30 ns (datasheet 30–57 ns) |
| Undershoot after a dominant bit | −0.65 V | −0.36 V |
| Loop delay TXD→RXD, rec→dom / dom→rec | 72.5 / 60 ns (max 140) | 86 / 107 ns (max 180) |
| Loop-delay skew | 13 ns, dominant shortened | 21 ns, dominant lengthened 13 ns (max 28) |
| 8 Mbps against the adapter | 2 bit errors, retried | 2 bit errors, retried; own loopback clean |
| Slew-rate-limited mode | yes, resistor on STBY | no |
| Standby | low-power receiver passes bus edges to RXD | ISO 11898-2:2016 wake-up pattern; RXD high until a valid WUP; bus biased to ground; 10 µA typ |
| Wake seen by the controller (E6) | 9 ms after the first frame | 3 ms after the first frame |
| Bus fault protection / common-mode range | ±40 V / ±25 V | ±58 V / ±30 V |
| TXD dominant timeout | yes | 1.2–4.0 ms |
| Price | ~3× | 1× |

Nothing in the bench or on the scope argues for the more expensive part. The TCAN3414DR's slower edges and smaller undershoot are the right direction for emissions on a real loom, which was the only EMC worry with the MAX33041E, and it needs no slew-limited mode to get there; its longer loop delay is absorbed by the controller's automatic TDC; its wider protection and common-mode range are simply better; and its standby needs nothing from the firmware that the old part did not.

## Agent bugs found on the way

The three fixed on the v3.1 bench (FD TSEG2 off by one at 2 and 4 Mbps, message-RAM overrun with timestamps on, one-shot FIFO reset) stayed fixed. One more was in the agent's one-shot abort detector: it read the TX FIFO's empty flag and then TXREQ as two separate SPI transactions, about 1 ms apart on the production bit-bang, and a frame in flight completing between the two reads looked exactly like an aborted one-shot message. The agent then reset a healthy FIFO, counted the frame as failed and skipped a sequence number, so every other frame of a burst could go missing with the slow SPI at some bit rates, always with TEC 0 and the host receiving exactly the frames the agent said it sent. That is the origin of the v3.1 report's unexplained "50 of 100 frames sent" in E1. The fix reads TXREQ first: hardware only clears it once the FIFO is empty, and nothing queues a frame between the two reads, so TXREQ-clear followed by not-empty can only be an abort. A production driver that polls for aborts needs the same ordering.

## Not testable on this bench

- Signal integrity on a real harness: stub reflections, ground offset between ECUs and emissions from the edges need the vehicle wiring or an EMC bench.
- Crystal ppm and drift over temperature. The tolerance sweep only shows the window is symmetric to within its ±0.3 % resolution; a frequency counter is needed for ppm figures, and temperature was not varied.
- Fault protection of the TCAN3414DR (±58 V bus standoff, shorts to battery or ground) and the NUP2105L clamping.
- Sleep and standby currents (10 µA typical for the transceiver in standby per its datasheet), and the draw from a real harness.
- Multi-node behaviour, high bus load from other nodes, wake-on-CAN with vehicle-specific frames (in particular whether a 1 Mbps bus's frames reliably satisfy the two-bit wake filter), and ISO 15765 / OBD, which the firmware does not implement yet.
- Bus-off recovery: the two-node rig never produced a data-field collision, with 400 same-ID frames from each end.
- 8 Mbps with a second 8 Mbps-rated transceiver: the adapter's 5 V part is the other end of this bus, so the two retried bit errors cannot be pinned on either side.
